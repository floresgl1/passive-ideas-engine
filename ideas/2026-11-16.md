# Daily Ideas — 2026-11-16
### 1. Utility-Bill Leak Watch for Small Landlords — [Passive]
- competence: unlabeled
- labeled_at: —

**Idea:** A subscription service where a small landlord forwards or uploads each unit's monthly water and gas bills (PDF), and it extracts usage, compares it against that unit's own weather-adjusted baseline, and sends one alert when a unit's consumption jumps in a way the weather doesn't explain (a running toilet, a slab leak, an unauthorized occupant).
**Leverages:** PDF ingestion from studyprof and voice-quiz, timezone-safe heterogeneous merges and feature engineering from finance_bot (bill periods joined to daily weather), and finance_bot's staleness detection and transition-only alerting so a landlord gets one alert per real change, not a monthly nag.
**One new thing to learn:** Weather normalization of utility usage with heating/cooling degree-days, so a cold month isn't flagged as a leak.
**Why it's worth it:** Owners of 2–30 units often pay the water bill themselves and only notice a leak when a $600 bill arrives months later; nobody reads every unit's bill against its own history. Once the extraction is built it runs unattended per bill cycle, and it has nothing to do with the current build.

### 2. Pickleball Serve Legality Checker — [Leverage]
- competence: unlabeled
- labeled_at: —

**Idea:** A phone app where a pickleball player records a few practice serves and gets each one checked against the volley-serve rules that can be measured from body pose (contact below the waist, an upward arm swing at contact), with the frame at contact shown and a legal/illegal verdict, sold as a one-time unlock.
**Leverages:** golf-swing-analyzer's single-phone-video pipeline: per-frame MediaPipe pose, phase segmentation to find the contact moment, scale-normalized body angles, the Flutter/ML Kit port and on-device inference, and its four-probe threshold tests for the legal/illegal boundary.
**One new thing to learn:** Detecting the moment of contact from audio (the paddle "pop") lined up with the video frames, since pose alone can't say when the ball was struck.
**Why it's worth it:** Players heading into their first tournaments get faulted on serves they've been hitting for years, and rec players argue about it constantly; the golf pipeline moves almost directly to a larger, fast-growing sport, the app runs entirely on-device with no server cost, and the verdict wording can stay honest ("measured waist line, not a referee's call").
